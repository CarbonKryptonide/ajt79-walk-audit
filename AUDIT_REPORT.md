# The exceptional prime 79 in the Alon–Jaeger–Tarsi conjecture

**Outcome.** The all-dimension theorem remains unproved in this investigation. The reset walk has enough one-coordinate entropy saving for $p=59,61,79$. The missing step is to realize that saving for a vector whose image in a quotient space is uniform. Two independent obstructions below explain why product measures and fixed-alphabet overlap counts do not supply this step. Nagy and Pach’s 2026 note explicitly includes $79$ among the unresolved cases. The earlier preprint containing the proposed Appendix C proof was withdrawn after the authors found an error in Claim 4.5. [2026 note, §1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.26320v1#S1) · [Withdrawal notice](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.03389)

## What the Lean kernel verified

- **Holding.** For the nine residues $0,1,2,3,7,14,44,63,78$, call the first three *low*. In the certified walk, two coordinates use one common fair sign. One coordinate must move while the other holds precisely at low residues, and their roles alternate. From the specified initial distribution their low probabilities alternate between $69/197$ and $49/197$. At both phases of **every** cycle the expected fraction of low coordinates is $59/197>21/100$. This is an admissible schedule in the full two-coordinate space. It refutes the asserted fixed-time marginal inference and leaves open the existence of a better schedule in a counterexample subspace. [Holding.lean](WalkAudit/Holding.lean)
- **Reset.** From zero take either sign of $1$. From $a\ne0$ take $0$ or $2a$, each with probability $1/2$. Lean verifies positive normalized stationary masses and $2H(\pi_p)<\log p$ over each of the three fields. In nats, the proof bounds $H(\pi_p)$ by $\log3+\frac43\log2$, giving $\exp(2H)\le 9\cdot2^{8/3}<59$. The completed Lake build reported only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound` for `Holding.holding_obstruction` and `Reset.reset_walk_audit`. ## A. Coupling the coordinates

Here is a sufficient condition with **no small-dimension exception**. A hypothetical counterexample yields a minimal collection of $N>0$ covering hyperplanes from the two bases. Let $T:\mathbb F_p^N\to Q$ send the $j$-th basis vector to the corresponding normal, with $Q$ their span and $W=\ker T$. Each of the two subfamilies is independent, so $r=\dim Q\ge N/2$. The kernel is *versatile*: for arbitrary nonzero strides $t_j$, and any specified $i$, it contains a move $d$ with $d_i=t_i$ and every $`d_j\in\{0,\pm t_j\}`$. To see this, scale the normals by the strides. Their difference-factor product in the integral group ring vanishes by the character criterion for a cover. Deleting factor $i$ leaves a nonzero product invariant under translation by the scaled normal $i$. Two nonzero coefficients separated by that translation give two subset sums of the other scaled normals. Their difference supplies the move. [Character criterion and group-ring reduction](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.03956)

It would suffice to construct $X$ with $TX$ uniform and every $X_j$ of reset law $\pi_p$. The contradiction would be exact:

$$
r\log p=H(TX)\le H(X)\le\sum_j H(X_j)=NH(\pi_p)<(N/2)\log p .
$$

Only the marginals after mixing the cosets uniformly are required.

**Independent coordinates and conditioning.** Every nonuniform rational law on $\mathbb F_p$ has nonzero Fourier coefficient at every nontrivial character. Indeed, its coefficient polynomial has rational coefficients and degree at most $p-1$. Vanishing at a primitive $p$-th root would force it to be a multiple of $1+z+\cdots+z^{p-1}$. For independent reset coordinates the Fourier coefficients multiply, so every nonzero quotient has nonuniform image. Conditioning the product law on each fiber also fails to preserve all the marginals. For a nonzero character represented by $b\in W^\perp$, choose $i$ with $b_i\ne0$. Given $X_i=a$, its expected value is $\zeta^{b_i a}\prod_{j\ne i}\widehat\pi_p(b_j)$, which varies with $a$. Thus $X_i$ and the quotient are dependent.

**Simultaneous moves and equal activation.** In the versatile hyperplane $\sum_{i=1}^{40}z_i=0$ over $\mathbb F_{79}$, take 39 strides equal to 1 and the last equal to 2. Any all-active signed sum is an odd integer of magnitude at most 41, hence cannot vanish modulo 79. Versatility for *every* stride vector follows from Cauchy–Davenport: the other 39 ternary sets have sumset size at least $\min(79,1+2\cdot39)=79$. This hyperplane has dimension 39 and tests the move rule alone.

A weaker repair would give each coordinate the same positive chance to move at each state. On a finite state space one could thin moves to a common constant rate. Any stationary law in each coset would then have one-coordinate marginals stationary for the reset kernel, whose stationary law is unique since every residue in these three fields is reachable from zero. This repair actually works at the particular stride vector above. Select two ordinary coordinates uniformly. With probability $2/39$, use both to compensate the last stride, and otherwise give the two opposite signs. Each coordinate moves with probability $2/39$.

The unresolved requirement is a **balanced distribution over legal supports** for every necessary state. Merely being able to activate each coordinate separately is insufficient. At unit strides the span of $(1,1,0,1,0)$ and $(1,-1,1,0,1)$ has exactly their two nonempty ternary supports for every prime $p\ge5$. Coordinates 3 and 4 force each span coefficient to be $0,\pm1$, and using both produces a forbidden $\pm2$. Every support therefore satisfies $m_1=m_3+m_4$ for its indicator $m$, precluding a positive common activation rate. This last example tests the single-state premise. It does not satisfy the full versatility hypothesis. Exploiting that additional hypothesis, or constructing the marginals directly across cosets, remains open here.

## B. Weighted and asymmetric counts

For fixed nonzero alphabets $A,B$, write $D_t=\mathrm{diag}(t)$. A scaling $t\in(\mathbb F_p^*)^n$ is bad if $Cu=D_tv$ for some $u\in A^n,v\in B^n$. Nagy–Pach use a **missing** scaling to obtain disjoint boxes and then delete a group-ring factor. Each pair gives at most one scaling. At $79$, the minimum midpoint size is 9 on both sides, giving $81^n$ pairs for $78^n$ scalings. Replacing a support test by vanishing of a signed weight alone would break the deletion argument. [Lemmas 4 and 10, Corollaries 2–3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.03956)

For example $`A=B=\{18,19,20,22,25,32,42,65,73\}`$ is invariant under $`\{1,23,55\}`$. Global scaling of $(u,v)$ saves a factor of three. At $n=2$ the identity matrix has 225 bad scalings, while $`\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)`$ has 1548, exceeding $81^2/3^2=729$. The saving does not repeat independently by coordinate. At $n=1$ the weight $v\mapsto v$ sums to zero modulo 79 on the entire intersection $A=B$ at $t=1$.

**A barrier to a uniform exponential improvement.** For any fixed $A,B\subset\mathbb F_p^*$ with $|A||B|>p$, there exist invertible matrices $C_n$ for which the fraction of bad scalings tends to one. Choose one representative of each projective line meeting $A^n$, obtaining $m\ge |A|^n/(p-1)$ vectors. For uniform $C$ among *all* matrices and fixed $t$, their images are pairwise independent and uniform. The number $Y$ landing in $D_tB^n$ has mean $\mu=m(|B|/p)^n$ and variance at most $\mu$. Consequently $\Pr(Y=0)\le1/\mu$. The probability of invertibility is $\prod_{i=1}^n(1-p^{-i})\ge1-1/(p-1)>0$ for $p>2$. Conditioning on invertibility costs a bounded factor. Averaging over $t$ now gives the assertion. In particular, no uniform estimate of the form $\mathrm{poly}(n)77^n$ for the number of bad scalings is possible for fixed nine-point alphabets at 79. The argument allows a single missing scaling, matrix-dependent choices of alphabets, or a stronger bound specific to minimal counterexamples.

Allowing a correlated alphabet $D\subset\mathbb F_{79}^k$ also has a limit. If every point admits a nontrivial midpoint move in *each coordinate alone* that stays in $D$ for both signs, every nonempty one-coordinate fiber has at least nine points. The projection away from that coordinate inherits the same property. Induction gives $|D|\ge9^k$. A smaller alphabet would have to exploit the actual moves in $W$.

## C. Lifting the group-ring identity

Write $P_M=\prod_i(1-g^{e_i})(1-g^{a_i})$. Conjecture 2 asks whether divisibility of **every** coefficient of $P_M$ by $p$ forces $P_M=0$ integrally. Complex Fourier evaluation detects integral vanishing exactly. The analogous componentwise divisibility test loses information: in $\mathbb Z[\zeta_p]$, each nonzero $1-\zeta_p^u$ has valuation one at $1-\zeta_p$, while $p$ has valuation $p-1$. Thus every character evaluation of $P_M$ is divisible by $p$ as soon as $2n\ge p-1$. Already $M=I_{39}$ at 79 passes every such test and has integral constant coefficient 1. Fourier inversion divides by $p^n$. [Conjecture 2 and Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.26320v1#S1)

Individual coefficients can also vanish modulo 79 without vanishing integrally. In dimension 80 take rows $e_i+e_{80}$ for $i<80$ and $e_{80}$ last. At exponent $(1,\dots,1,3)$ the coefficient is

$$
-\mathrm{coeff}_{z^3}\\!\left((1+z)^{79}(1-z)^2\right)=-72{,}996=-79\cdot924.
$$

Selecting exponent 1 in each of the first 79 coordinates gives precisely the factor $-(1+z)$ in each, and degree 81 prevents wraparound at exponent 3. The constant coefficient is 1. Likewise the invertible two-dimensional matrix in §B has zero permanent, defeating a proof from the squarefree coefficient of the product of its two row forms. A lift would need a relation among all coefficients or a guarantee that some coefficient survives.
